## 2. The Unique Challenges of Talking-Head Content

A **talking-head video** puts the creator front and center with nowhere to hide. If the script is poorly written, the viewer notices immediately:

1. **The 'Reading-Robot' Trap:** When scripts are written like written articles or formal essays, the talent sounds stiff. Their eyes dart side-to-side reading the prompter, and the human connection with the audience is broken.
2. **Talking-Head Monotony (Detik ke-4 Drop-Off):** Having a person talk continuously to a static camera without angle cuts or visual cues causes visual fatigue in 3 to 4 seconds. Penonton cepat bosan jika layar tidak bergerak.
3. **Breathless Delivery:** Sentences with too many clauses force the speaker to rush or run out of breath, making the delivery feel stressful rather than confident.

Our workflow solves these problems by writing **for the human voice and camera lens simultaneously**.

## 3. Four Core Rules for Talking-Head Scripts

```text
[00:00 - 00:03] The Hook: Stop the thumb with an immediate insight or relatable dilemma.
       │
[00:04 - 00:15] The Agitation: Validate why this matters to the viewer right now.
       │
[00:16 - 00:48] The Core Insight: 2 to 3 clear, practical takeaways without filler.
       │
[00:49 - 00:60] The Close & Loop: Gentle call-to-action or a seamless wrap-around loop.
```

### 1. Spoken Dialect & Breath Pacing (130 to 145 WPM)
We write using the rhythm of real conversational speech:
* **Short breath-units:** Sentences stay under 12 to 15 words so the speaker can breathe naturally.
* **Natural spoken connectors:** We replace stiff bookish transitions (*"namun demikian"*, *"adapun"*) with natural spoken phrasing (*"nah"*, *"tapi faktanya"*, *"kuncinya cuma satu"*).
* **Word budgets:** 60-second video = 130 to 145 words. 30-second video = 65 to 75 words.

### 2. Teleprompter-Friendly Chunking
To keep the speaker looking directly into the camera lens rather than scanning left-to-right, scripts are broken into narrow 4-to-6 word visual phrases. This keeps eye movement minimal and maintains warm eye contact with the viewer.

### 3. Curing Talking-Head Fatigue with 2-Column AV Formatting
To keep a talking-head video dynamic, we provide a two-column Audio-Visual table:
* **Left Column (Spoken Dialogue):** The exact words for the talent, with bold highlights on words that need vocal emphasis.
* **Right Column (Visual & Editor Direction):** Cues for camera reframing (e.g., *punch-in zoom 110%*), pop-up text keywords, sound effects, or short B-roll overlays every 3 to 4 seconds.

### 4. Seamless Loop Closures
The final sentence is designed to connect smoothly back into the opening hook, encouraging viewers to watch the video on loop.

## 6. How We Work with Creators & Brands

1. **Voice & Tone Discovery:** We review your existing videos and discuss your natural speaking style, slang preferences, and comfort zone so the script sounds 100% authentically like you.
2. **Hook Selection:** For each video topic, we provide 2 to 3 hook choices so you can choose the one that feels most natural to deliver.
3. **Turnkey AV Naskah:** Delivered in Google Docs, Notion, or teleprompter app formats (CapCut, PromptSmart, etc.) alongside clear cues for your editor.
4. **Low-Risk Trial:** We usually start with 1 trial script to calibrate tone and delivery before moving into batch content.
